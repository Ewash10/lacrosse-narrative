The Premier Lacrosse League (PLL)

Founded in 2018 by Paul and Mike Rabil as a player-first alternative to Major League Lacrosse, the Premier Lacrosse League modernized professional field lacrosse through an equity-sharing model, comprehensive health benefits, and a high-energy tour-based structure before ultimately absorbing the MLL in a 2020 merger. The league gained mainstream visibility through a multi-year media rights deal across ESPN platforms, which dramatically boosted national viewership and broadcast production values with in-game player access and dedicated network windows. This institutional growth significantly improved player economics, lifting base salaries into the $35,000 to $40,000 range alongside performance bonuses, winter tournament prize pools, and equity incentives. By the conclusion of the 2026 season, the PLL had strategically solidified its transition into home-market identities—anchoring clubs to specific regions while maintaining its successful traveling weekend format—and positioned itself as an essential developmental runway for the fast-paced sixes discipline ahead of the sport's inclusion in the Los Angeles 2028 Olympic Games.
